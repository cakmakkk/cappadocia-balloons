It happens to plenty of visitors. The alarm goes off before dawn, the minibus picks you up, and then the word comes that nobody is flying today. Here is why, and what you can do about it.

## Why flights are cancelled

Balloons can only take off in calm, clear air. Wind is the usual reason for a red flag, followed by rain, fog and poor visibility. Cancellations happen in every season, but they are more common in winter.

The decision is made for the whole area, not for one company. When the flags are red, nobody flies, whoever you booked with.

## What happens to your booking

When the authorities cancel a morning, companies usually offer a seat on another morning or a refund. The details depend on the company and on where you booked, so read the cancellation terms before you pay, and ask what happens if you can't fly on any morning of your stay.

## Leave yourself a spare morning

- Book the flight for the first morning of your stay. If it's called off, you still have the following mornings.
- If the balloons are the main reason for your trip, stay at least two or three nights.
- Look at the [outlook for the coming mornings](../../tomorrow/) when you pick a date. It is only a weather forecast, but calm mornings are the ones that fly.

## When the balloons stay down

A grounded morning in Cappadocia can still be a good one:

- Walk one of the valleys near Göreme at sunrise, such as Rose Valley or Love Valley.
- Visit the Göreme Open-Air Museum and its rock-cut churches.
- Go underground at Kaymaklı or Derinkuyu, two of the region's underground cities.
